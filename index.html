The Weekly Specimen · Vol. 12

Foundry

In which two typefaces meet, size each other up, and decide they could probably share a page.

Good typography is invisible until it isn’t. The reader should glide from line to line without noticing the leading, the tracking or the careful weight of each stroke. When a pairing works, the headline sets the mood and the body text does the patient work of being read. Try loosening the leading for long passages and tightening the tracking on big display sizes — large type tends to look gappy, small type tends to look cramped.
